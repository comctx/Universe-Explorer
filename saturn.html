Understand Saturn

Switch between a simple explanation and deeper planetary science.

Saturn is the sixth planet from the Sun. It is a giant world made mostly of hydrogen and helium, with no solid surface you could stand on. Its famous rings are mostly water ice mixed with smaller amounts of rock and dust.

Saturn's Rings

Explore the A, B, and C rings, the Cassini Division, composition, and the extreme thinness of the system.

What the rings are made of
Mostly water ice
Rocky material
Dust and darker particles
Thin compared with their width

The main ring system stretches hundreds of thousands of kilometers across, yet much of it is only tens of meters thick. If the rings were the width of a football field, they would be far thinner than a sheet of paper.

Major Moons

Seven important moons, with size, orbital distance, orbital speed, and a reason each one matters.

Titan

Diameter: 5,150 kmDistance: 1,221,870 kmOrbit speed: ~5.57 km/s

Larger than Mercury, with a thick atmosphere and lakes and seas of liquid methane and ethane.

Enceladus

Diameter: 504 kmDistance: 237,948 kmOrbit speed: ~12.63 km/s

Ice geysers reveal a subsurface ocean, making Enceladus one of the most compelling places to search for habitable conditions.

Mimas

Diameter: 396 kmDistance: 185,539 kmOrbit speed: ~14.3 km/s

Its huge Herschel crater makes it look strikingly like a fictional battle station.

Rhea

Diameter: 1,528 kmDistance: 527,108 kmOrbit speed: ~8.48 km/s

An icy, heavily cratered moon and Saturn's second-largest satellite.

Dione

Diameter: 1,123 kmDistance: 377,396 kmOrbit speed: ~10.0 km/s

Bright ice cliffs and fractures cross a surface that records a long geologic history.

Tethys

Diameter: 1,062 kmDistance: 294,619 kmOrbit speed: ~11.35 km/s

A low-density icy moon with the enormous Odysseus crater and the Ithaca Chasma canyon system.

Iapetus

Diameter: 1,469 kmDistance: 3,560,820 kmOrbit speed: ~3.26 km/s

Known for its dramatic two-tone surface and a remarkable ridge running along much of its equator.

Exploration Missions

From the first close flyby to years in orbit around Saturn.

1979

Pioneer 11

First spacecraft to encounter Saturn up close and the first to fly through the planet's ring plane.

1980

Voyager 1

Returned detailed images of Saturn and Titan and made a close Titan flyby.

1981

Voyager 2

Studied Saturn, its atmosphere, rings, and moons before continuing to Uranus and Neptune.

2004–2017

Cassini–Huygens

Cassini orbited Saturn for 13 years. Huygens landed on Titan in 2005.

Educational Visualizations

Five ways to understand how the Saturn system works.

1. Ring Formation Theories

Scientists are still refining when and how Saturn's rings formed.

A moon or icy body could have crossed inside Saturn's Roche limit and been torn apart by tidal forces, spreading debris into orbit.

2. Moon Orbits

Inner moons orbit faster; outer moons take much longer to complete a circuit.

3. Magnetosphere

Saturn's magnetic field creates a huge cavity in the solar wind.

4. Seasonal Changes

Saturn is tilted about 26.7°, so its rings appear more open or more edge-on during its long seasons.

5. Internal Structure

Saturn has no sharp solid surface. The atmosphere gradually becomes denser and transitions into deeper layers under enormous pressure.
