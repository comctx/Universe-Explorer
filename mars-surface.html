What Would It Feel Like?

You Would Feel Lighter

Mars has about 38% of Earth's surface gravity. A person who weighs 180 pounds on Earth would press on the ground with roughly the same force as about 68 pounds on Mars.

You Could Not Breathe

The atmosphere is extremely thin and mostly carbon dioxide. A human explorer would need a pressurized suit and life support.

The Sky Looks Different

Fine dust gives much of the daytime sky a dusty tan or reddish look. Near sunset, the region around the Sun can appear bluish.

Dust Is Everywhere

Very fine iron-rich dust coats much of the surface and can affect mechanisms, solar panels, visibility, and thermal control.

Temperatures Swing

Temperature changes greatly with season, location, elevation, weather, and time of day. Mars is generally far colder than Earth.

Water Is Mostly Ice

Liquid water is generally unstable on the present-day surface, but Mars contains large quantities of water ice.

Technical — Mars Entry & Environment

Why entry is difficult

Mars has enough atmosphere to create extreme heating and strong aerodynamic forces at several kilometers per second, but not enough atmosphere to slow a heavy vehicle safely all the way to the ground. That is why a realistic landing sequence still needs additional braking.

Thin atmosphere

The atmosphere is dominated by carbon dioxide, with smaller amounts of nitrogen and argon plus trace gases.

Surface geology

Mars contains volcanic plains, impact craters, sedimentary deposits, ancient river valleys, giant volcanoes, canyon systems, dunes, and polar ice.

Advanced — What the Simulation Is Showing

Entry corridor

The arriving vehicle must enter the atmosphere at the correct angle. Too shallow and it may skip back out; too steep and heating and deceleration become much more severe.

Peak heating and peak deceleration

Heating and G-load do not peak at the same instant. The simulation separates the bright plasma phase from the strongest braking phase to make that distinction visible.

Powered descent

Near the ground, rocket thrust becomes the dominant control in this generic heavy-lander sequence. The dust cloud grows rapidly because exhaust interacts with the loose Martian regolith.

Radiation

Mars lacks Earth's thick atmosphere and strong global magnetic field. Long-duration human missions would require substantial radiation protection.
