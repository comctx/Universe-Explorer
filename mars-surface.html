What Would It Feel Like?

You Would Feel Lighter

Mars has about 38% of Earth's surface gravity. A person who weighs 180 pounds on Earth would press on the ground with roughly the same force as about 68 pounds on Mars.

You Could Not Breathe

The atmosphere is extremely thin and mostly carbon dioxide. A human explorer would need a pressurized suit and life-support system.

The Sky Looks Different

Fine dust gives much of the daytime sky a dusty tan or reddish look. Near sunset, the region around the Sun can appear bluish.

Dust Is Everywhere

Very fine iron-rich dust coats much of the surface and can affect mechanisms, solar panels, visibility, and thermal control.

Temperatures Swing

Temperature changes greatly with season, location, elevation, weather, and time of day. Mars is generally far colder than Earth.

Water Is Mostly Ice

Liquid water is generally unstable on the present-day surface, but Mars contains large quantities of water ice.

Technical — Martian Environment

Atmospheric Entry

Mars has enough atmosphere to create severe heating and aerodynamic drag during high-speed entry, but it is much too thin to make landing easy. A landing system must still remove a large amount of velocity near the surface.

Atmosphere

The atmosphere is dominated by carbon dioxide, with smaller amounts of nitrogen and argon plus trace gases.

Geology

Mars contains basaltic volcanic terrain, impact craters, sedimentary deposits, ancient river valleys, enormous volcanoes, canyon systems, dunes, and polar ice deposits.

Advanced — Why Mars Looks This Way

Iron Oxides

The reddish color comes largely from iron-bearing minerals oxidized and mixed into fine surface dust.

Atmospheric Loss

Mars once had a thicker atmosphere. Over geological time it lost much of that atmosphere through several escape processes and interaction with the solar wind.

Radiation

Mars lacks Earth's thick atmosphere and strong global magnetic field. Long-duration human missions would require substantial radiation protection.

Ancient Water

Orbital observations and landed missions show abundant evidence that liquid water shaped ancient Mars, including valleys, deltas, lake deposits, and water-altered minerals.
