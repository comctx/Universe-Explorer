Visit Famous Locations

Jezero Crater
Jezero contains an ancient river delta and evidence that a lake existed here billions of years ago. NASA's Perseverance rover landed in Jezero in 2021 to study its geology and search for signs of ancient habitable environments.

What Would It Feel Like?

You Would Feel Lighter

Mars has only about 38% of Earth's surface gravity. A person who weighs 180 pounds on Earth would press on the ground with roughly the same force as about 68 pounds on Mars.

You Could Not Breathe

The atmosphere is extremely thin and is mostly carbon dioxide. Humans would require a pressurized suit and life-support system.

The Sky Looks Different

Fine Martian dust changes the color of the sky. Daytime skies often appear dusty reddish or tan, while sunsets near the Sun can appear bluish.

Dust Is Everywhere

Very fine iron-rich dust coats much of the surface. Dust can affect solar panels, mechanisms, visibility, thermal control, and equipment.

Temperatures Swing

Temperatures vary greatly with location, season, time of day, elevation, and weather. Mars is generally far colder than Earth.

No Open Water

Liquid water is generally unstable on the present surface because of the low pressure and temperature, although Mars contains large quantities of water ice.

Technical — Martian Environment

Atmosphere

Mars has a thin atmosphere dominated by carbon dioxide, with smaller amounts of nitrogen, argon, oxygen, carbon monoxide and trace gases.

Pressure

Typical surface pressure is only a small fraction of Earth's sea-level atmospheric pressure and varies significantly with elevation and season.

Geology

Mars contains basaltic volcanic terrain, impact craters, sedimentary deposits, ancient river valleys, enormous volcanoes, canyon systems and polar ice deposits.

Advanced — Why Mars Looks This Way

Iron Oxides

The reddish appearance of much of Mars comes from iron-bearing minerals that oxidized and became incorporated into fine surface dust.

Atmospheric Loss

Mars once had a thicker atmosphere. Over geological time, the planet lost much of it through several processes, including interaction with the solar wind and atmospheric escape.

Radiation

Mars lacks Earth's thick atmosphere and strong global magnetic field. Future human explorers would require significant protection from solar energetic particles and galactic cosmic radiation.

Ancient Water

Orbital observations and rover missions have found abundant evidence that liquid water shaped the ancient Martian surface, including valleys, deltas, lake deposits and water-altered minerals.
