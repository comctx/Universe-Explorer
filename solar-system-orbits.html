Why not fly straight to Mars?

A spacecraft leaving Earth is already moving around the Sun. The efficient route is another solar orbit that intersects Mars later.

Straight Line ✕

Mars keeps moving while the spacecraft travels. Aiming at where Mars is at launch would miss the planet, and fighting orbital motion would require far more energy.

Hohmann Transfer ✓

The spacecraft enters an elliptical orbit around the Sun and reaches Mars' orbital distance near the same time Mars reaches the intercept point.

Planet Orbital Speed Comparison

The closer a planet is to the Sun, the faster it generally travels through its orbit.

What this display teaches

Inner planets orbit faster because they are closer to the Sun. Outer planets have much longer years. Mars also follows a slightly elliptical path rather than a perfect circle, which is visible here more clearly than Earth's nearly circular orbit. Turn on the Inner Planets view to spread Mercury through Mars apart, watch the asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter, and use the orbit trails to compare how quickly the planets sweep around the Sun. You can also display the approximate habitable zone, Kuiper Belt, minor worlds, the Earth-to-Mars launch phase angle, and a straight-line-versus-Hohmann comparison.
