Technical View

Transfer Orbit

A conventional efficient Mars mission can use an elliptical heliocentric transfer orbit rather than flying directly from Earth to Mars.

Launch Geometry

In this simplified Hohmann model, Mars is about 44° ahead of Earth at departure so that spacecraft and planet reach the same point at arrival.

Travel Time

The idealized Earth-to-Mars Hohmann transfer takes about 259 days. Actual missions vary because real trajectories include launch conditions, navigation, corrections and mission requirements.

Transfer-orbit approximation
a = (rEarth + rMars) / 2
Transfer time ≈ ½ × orbital period of the transfer ellipse
v = √[ μSun × (2/r − 1/a) ]

The last expression is the vis-viva equation used here to estimate the spacecraft's heliocentric speed along the transfer ellipse.

Advanced Relativity

Time Dilation

A traveler moving at relativistic speed accumulates less proper time than an observer in the Earth frame.

Length Contraction

In the traveler's inertial frame, the distance along the direction of motion is shorter than the corresponding Earth-frame distance.

No Photon Rest Frame

The app does not calculate a traveler's elapsed time at exactly c. Special relativity does not define a valid rest frame for a photon.

Special-relativity equations
γ = 1 / √(1 − v²/c²)
Δτ = Δt / γ
Ltraveler = LEarth / γ

Choose a relativistic travel mode to see the equations applied.

The star-streak and visual approach effects are illustrative. A fully accurate relativistic view requires ray tracing, aberration, Doppler shifting and intensity transformations that are beyond this first prototype.

What This Prototype Teaches

Mars Is Moving

Spaceflight is an interception problem. Earth, Mars and the spacecraft all continue moving around the Sun.

Distance Is Enormous

Even neighboring planets can be tens or hundreds of millions of kilometers apart.

Relativity Changes Time

As speed approaches the speed of light, the difference between Earth-frame time and traveler proper time becomes dramatic.
